Is Goldfelty safe?

Last updated 26 October 2026

Goldfelty is a portfolio tracker, not a wallet. It never sees your recovery phrase or private keys, it can’t move anything without your own wallet asking you first, and all of its source code is public. This page explains exactly what it can and can’t do, and how to check a download for yourself before you open it.

What Goldfelty never does

  • Ask for your recovery phrase or private key. Not in the app, not on this site, not by email. Anyone who asks for one is a scammer, even if they say they’re Goldfelty.
  • Ask you to approve token spending. Goldfelty never requests an “approve”, “permit” or “set approval for all” signature, the kind wallet drainers use. It only sends the exact transfer you reviewed.
  • Hold your funds. Your crypto stays in your wallets the whole time.
  • Track you. The app has no analytics, ads or crash reporting.

What your wallet will ask you to approve

  • Transfers you start yourself on the Send screen, showing the recipient and amount you entered. When a fee applies (a fixed 0.5% of tokens sent through Goldfelty), it’s shown on the review screen first, and your wallet shows it too.
  • One plain-text message, only if you create an optional account, to prove the address is yours. A message signature can’t move funds.

Goldfelty also checks what your wallet signed against what you reviewed. On Bitcoin, Solana and Tron it does that before sending, and won’t send if anything differs. On Ethereum and similar networks your wallet sends the transaction itself, and Goldfelty flags it in your activity if it doesn’t match.

Checking a download

Only download Goldfelty from this site or from its GitHub Releases page. Every installer is built by GitHub’s servers straight from the public source code, and you can check that yourself:

  • Each release lists every file’s SHA-256 checksum in SHA256SUMS.txt.
  • Each file carries a build provenance attestation, a signed public record of the exact commit and workflow that built it. Check one with gh attestation verify <file> --repo gceoale/goldfelty-portfolio-manager.
  • You can also upload any installer to VirusTotal to scan it.

Why does my computer warn me?

Goldfelty’s builds aren’t signed by Apple or Microsoft yet, which takes paid certificates from each. Once they are, these warnings go away. An unsigned app isn’t the same as malware, but it does mean you should check the download as above before opening it.

Windows: SmartScreen shows “Windows protected your PC”. Choose More info, then Run anyway.

macOS: open the app once, then go to System Settings, Privacy & Security, and choose Open Anyway. If macOS instead says the app “is damaged”, that’s how it treats unsigned apps downloaded from the internet. The steps for that appear when you choose a Mac download on the home page.

Open source and reviewed

The full source code is on GitHub, along with a security review of the app, the wallet connection and the account service. Fee payments are public on each blockchain, like every transaction.
